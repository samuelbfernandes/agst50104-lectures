Welcome to AGST 50104!

  • Experimental Design is the foundation of scientific inquiry
  • You will learn to design rigorous experiments and analyze results correctly
  • This course applies to agriculture, horticulture, food science, animal science, soil science, and plant breeding
  • We focus on practical applications in your discipline
  • By the end, you’ll be confident designing your own experiments

Key points: - Emphasize relevance to their fields - Reassure everyone that we build from fundamentals - Set tone of collaborative learning

Homework & Teamwork

How Homework Works:

  • Groups of ~5 students (formed Week 1)
  • Assigned almost every Friday
  • Due Thursday 11:59 PM (written submission)
  • Friday Lab: One member randomly selected to present
  • Lowest homework score is dropped at end of semester

Grading is 60% group + 20% your presentation + 20% peer evaluation

Your individual grade depends on effort AND how you’re perceived by teammates.

Team Success Tips:

  • Communicate early and often
  • Divide work fairly
  • Submit Thursday, not Friday
  • Practice explaining your work
  • Use AI (GitHub Copilot) to learn faster
  • Office hours are for team troubleshooting

Timing: ~4 minutes

Story: “Group work teaches you real research—you’ll do this in industry or grad school. Start strong!”

The Final Project

Project Requirements:

  • Topic: Randomly assigned in Week 1 (one of 9–11 experimental designs)
  • Format: 9–11 minute video presentation (unlisted YouTube)
  • Deadline: April 24, 2026, 11:59 PM
  • Grading: 200 points = 50% concept explanation + 50% applied example
  • All group members must appear in the video to receive a grade

Projects showcase REAL experimental design thinking—not just formulas!

What to Include:

  • Clear explanation of your assigned design
  • Why it’s appropriate for the scenario
  • Complete analysis workflow:
    • Data exploration
    • Statistical analysis
    • Diagnostics
    • Conclusions
  • Visualizations that tell the story

Timing: ~4 minutes

Guidance: “Your project is a portfolio piece. Make it polished!”

Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, you will:

  1. Design rigorous experiments (CRD, RCBD, factorial, split-plot, etc.)
  2. Choose appropriate designs based on constraints and research questions
  3. Analyze data using ANOVA, linear mixed models, and GLMs
  4. Interpret statistical results in practical, domain-relevant language
  5. Communicate results clearly (reports, presentations, visualizations)
  6. Recognize and avoid common pitfalls (confounding, pseudoreplication, incorrect error terms)
  7. Use R & Quarto for reproducible, professional analyses

These skills apply to your research, graduate work, and professional career.

Timing: ~3 minutes

Motivation: “These are the tools and concepts that professional researchers use every day.”
